# Notes: Alert Triage & Severity Prioritization

## Why Triage Matters
In a Security Operations Center (SOC), alerts come in constantly. Most alerts generated by SIEM rules or EDR tools are noise or false positives. Triage is the process of quickly reviewing incoming alerts to determine if they represent a genuine security incident or safe activity. As a Tier 1 analyst, my job is to filter the noise so the team can focus on real threats.

## Severity vs. Priority
One of the first traps to avoid is treating severity and priority as the same thing:
- **Severity (Technical):** How bad or dangerous the activity looks on paper. (e.g., Ransomware execution, Brute force attack, Cobalt Strike beaconing). This is usually assigned by the detection rule.
- **Priority (Business Impact):** Combines technical severity with context about *what* is affected. An alert on an isolated test server has low business priority, whereas the exact same alert on a domain controller or payment server has critical priority.

## Context Collection
Before making a decision on any alert, I need to look at the core telemetry pieces:
1. **Source:** Where is the activity coming from? (External IP, internal workstation, service account?)
2. **Target:** What asset is being attacked or accessed? (Workstation, server, database?)
3. **Timeline:** When did this happen? Is it a single isolated event or part of a multi-stage attack chain?
4. **Affected Asset & User:** Who owns the machine or account? Is it a standard user or an admin?

## What Tier 1 Needs Before Escalating
If I decide an alert is malicious or suspicious enough to escalate to Tier 2, I cannot just dump a ticket link and say "look at this." I need to provide:
- Clear evidence showing why I believe it's real.
- The full timeline of events.
- Source and destination details.
- Summary of initial validation steps I already performed (so Tier 2 doesn't waste time repeating my work).

## False Positives & Alert Fatigue
- **False Positives (FPs):** Legitimate activity that triggers a security rule (e.g., a vulnerability scanner triggering a port scan alert).
- **Alert Fatigue:** When analysts become overwhelmed by the sheer volume of alerts (especially noisy FPs), leading to missed real incidents. Good tuning and structured triage help combat this.

## Key Takeaways
- Always check the asset criticality—severity alone is misleading.
- Don't close an alert blindly without checking surrounding context logs.
- Document clearly during triage; if it's not documented, it didn't happen.
